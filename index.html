Seven Steps of Moral Reasoning

Using Scott Rae's framework to work through a tough choice.

You witness a classmate cheating on an online exam. Do you report it or stay silent?

Use the arrows, swipe, or your keyboard to begin ✿

1

Gather the Facts

What happened? Is there a clear ethical issue?

During an online exam, I see a classmate using notes or another device when they shouldn't.

I don't know their full situation, but the issue is clear: this is academic dishonesty.

2

Determine the Ethical Issues

What are the moral concerns involved?

Several values pull in different directions here.

Fairness to honest students Honesty and integrity Loyalty to a classmate Responsibility to my school
3

Choose Relevant Principles

What ethical values or principles apply?

These are the values I want my decision to respect.

Honesty Fairness Responsibility Compassion Respect for rules
4

List the Alternatives

What choices do I have? (Tap a card to open it)

A. Report it to the teacher

I tell the teacher directly what I saw.

B. Stay silent

I say nothing and focus on my own exam.

C. Talk to the classmate first

I speak with them privately and ask them to make it right.

D. Report anonymously

I let the teacher know without giving my name.

5

Compare Alternatives with Principles

Which options align with ethical values? (Tap to see)

Stay silent

Conflicts with fairness and honesty. It only protects loyalty.

Weak fit
Report it

Upholds fairness and honesty, but may cost loyalty and compassion.

Partial fit
Talk to them first

Balances honesty with compassion and gives them a chance to correct it.

Strong fit
Report anonymously

Protects fairness, and shields me from conflict, but skips the chance to help.

Partial fit
6

Assess the Consequences

What are the possible outcomes of each choice? (Tap to see)

If I report

The classmate may be penalized and the friendship strained, but grades stay fair.

If I stay silent

No conflict for me, but the unfairness continues and cheating may spread.

If I talk first

It could be resolved kindly, or they might ignore me and I'd need a next step.

If I report anonymously

Fairness is protected, but the classmate never hears from me directly.

7

Make a Decision

What will I do and why?

I would talk to my classmate privately first. If they refuse to stop, I would report it to the teacher.

Fairness matters to everyone taking the exam, and I believe people deserve a chance to fix their mistake.
